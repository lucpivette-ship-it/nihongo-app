# Grammar — N5 Batch 2 (20 points)

## ni/e — to (direction)
**JLPT:** N5

Both に and へ can mark a destination ("to"), though へ has a slightly more literary, "toward" nuance.

**JP:** 明日、東京へ行きます。
**Reading:** Ashita, Tokyo e ikimasu.
**EN:** I'm going to Tokyo tomorrow.

## no — possessive particle
**JLPT:** N5

Marks possession between two nouns, similar to "'s" in English: "[A] の [B]" means "[B] of [A]."

**JP:** これは私の本です。
**Reading:** Kore wa watashi no hon desu.
**EN:** This is my book.

## no desu — explanatory emphasis (written form of んです)
**JLPT:** N5

More formal/written equivalent of んです, used to add explanatory nuance.

**JP:** 電車が遅れているのです。
**Reading:** Densha ga okurete iru no desu.
**EN:** (You see,) the train is delayed.

## no ga heta — to be bad at ~
**JLPT:** N5

Attaches to the dictionary form of a verb + のが下手 to say someone is bad at doing something.

**JP:** 私は歌うのが下手です。
**Reading:** Watashi wa utau no ga heta desu.
**EN:** I'm bad at singing.

## no ga jouzu — to be good at ~
**JLPT:** N5

Attaches to the dictionary form of a verb + のが上手 to say someone is good at doing something.

**JP:** 兄は料理をするのが上手です。
**Reading:** Ani wa ryouri o suru no ga jouzu desu.
**EN:** My older brother is good at cooking.

## no ga suki — to like doing ~
**JLPT:** N5

Attaches to the dictionary form of a verb + のが好き to say someone likes doing something.

**JP:** 私は本を読むのが好きです。
**Reading:** Watashi wa hon o yomu no ga suki desu.
**EN:** I like reading books.

## no naka de [A] ga ichiban — out of [group], [A] is the most/best
**JLPT:** N5

Used to single out the best/most of something within a group: の中で marks the group, and 一番 means "the most."

**JP:** 果物の中でりんごが一番好きです。
**Reading:** Kudamono no naka de ringo ga ichiban suki desu.
**EN:** Of all fruits, I like apples the most.

## o/go — honorific prefix
**JLPT:** N5

Polite prefix attached to nouns (お for native Japanese words, ご for Sino-Japanese words) to add politeness or respect.

**JP:** お名前は何ですか。
**Reading:** Onamae wa nan desu ka.
**EN:** What is your name? (polite)

## o kudasai — please give me ~
**JLPT:** N5

Attaches to a noun + をください to politely request an item.

**JP:** 水をください。
**Reading:** Mizu o kudasai.
**EN:** Water, please.

## shikashi — but; however
**JLPT:** N5

Formal conjunction used at the start of a sentence to contrast with the previous statement.

**JP:** 天気は悪かった。しかし、試合は行われた。
**Reading:** Tenki wa warukatta. Shikashi, shiai wa okonawareta.
**EN:** The weather was bad. However, the match took place.

## sore kara — and then; after that
**JLPT:** N5

Conjunction connecting two sequential actions or events, "and then" / "after that."

**JP:** 宿題をしました。それから、テレビを見ました。
**Reading:** Shukudai o shimashita. Sorekara, terebi o mimashita.
**EN:** I did my homework. After that, I watched TV.

## soshite — and; and then
**JLPT:** N5

Conjunction linking sentences or items in sequence, meaning "and" or "and then."

**JP:** 朝ごはんを食べました。そして、学校へ行きました。
**Reading:** Asagohan o tabemashita. Soshite, gakkou e ikimashita.
**EN:** I ate breakfast. And then I went to school.

## sugiru — too much; excessively ~
**JLPT:** N5

Attaches to the ます-stem of verbs or the stem of adjectives to mean "too much ~" or "excessively ~."

**JP:** このケーキは甘すぎます。
**Reading:** Kono keeki wa amasugimasu.
**EN:** This cake is too sweet.

## te aru — is/has been done (resulting state)
**JLPT:** N5

Attaches to the て-form of a transitive verb to describe a resulting state from a deliberate action, often implying someone prepared it.

**JP:** ドアが開けてあります。
**Reading:** Doa ga akete arimasu.
**EN:** The door has been left open (on purpose).

## te kara — after doing ~
**JLPT:** N5

Attaches to the て-form of a verb to mean "after doing [A]," emphasizing the sequence of events.

**JP:** 宿題をしてから、遊びます。
**Reading:** Shukudai o shite kara, asobimasu.
**EN:** I'll play after doing my homework.

## wa~yori...desu — [A] is more ~ than [B]
**JLPT:** N5

Comparative structure: "[Noun A] wa [Noun B] yori ... desu" means "[A] is more ... than [B]."

**JP:** 私は兄より背が高いです。
**Reading:** Watashi wa ani yori se ga takai desu.
**EN:** I am taller than my older brother.

## wa dou desu ka — how about ~?
**JLPT:** N5

Used to ask for someone's opinion or to make a suggestion about a topic.

**JP:** このレストランはどうですか。
**Reading:** Kono resutoran wa dou desu ka.
**EN:** How about this restaurant?

## ya — and, or (incomplete list connector)
**JLPT:** N5

Connects nouns in a list that is not exhaustive, implying "and others," unlike と which lists items completely.

**JP:** 机の上に本やペンがあります。
**Reading:** Tsukue no ue ni hon ya pen ga arimasu.
**EN:** There are things like books and pens on the desk (among other things).

## yo — emphasis ending particle
**JLPT:** N5

Sentence-ending particle used to add emphasis or share new information the listener may not know.

**JP:** このケーキ、おいしいですよ。
**Reading:** Kono keeki, oishii desu yo.
**EN:** This cake is delicious, you know!

## yori~hou ga — [B] is more ~ than [A]
**JLPT:** N5

Alternate word order for comparisons: places the compared-against item first with より, and the preferred item's category with ほうが.

**JP:** バスより電車のほうが速いです。
**Reading:** Basu yori densha no hou ga hayai desu.
**EN:** The train is faster than the bus.
